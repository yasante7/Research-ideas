# Literature Review — The Enterprise Dimension

Search across Consensus (220m papers), plus working-paper and grey-literature search. Organised by what each body of work does to the proposal.

---

## 1. "Technology is not enough" is already established. Don't claim it as the finding.

The productive-use-of-energy (PUE) literature has repeatedly shown that energy access alone does not improve enterprise performance.

- **Pueyo et al. (2018), Kenya.** Difference-in-differences on solar mini-grids: nearly two years after installation, **no improvement in business performance**. Electricity consumption stayed low, demand for local products did not pick up. Recommends complementary measures on agricultural productivity and market access. → [The impact of solar mini-grids on Kenya's rural enterprises](https://consensus.app/papers/details/432e4937764a589083b57cb9065e310b/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Pelz et al. (2021), North India.** Of 2,004 micro- and small enterprises in near-fully electrified villages, **34% had no grid connection at all**, and **75% of those connected consumed under 1 kWh/day — lighting and fans only**. Connection tracked wealth, not supply quality. This is the conversion gap, quantified, in another setting. → [Electrification and productive use among micro- and small-enterprises in rural North India](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6fdfdee6b0a358d7a2c39bc53f2596ad/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Neelsen & Peters (2011), Uganda.** 200 micro-enterprises on Lake Victoria: electrification altered the sectoral mix and capital used, but produced **no expansionary effect on profits or wages**. → [Electricity usage in micro-enterprises — Evidence from Lake Victoria, Uganda](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d87dd93008e85ef5a46616d2cb45d82f/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Aarakit et al. (2024), systematic review of 54 studies.** Concludes that electricity factors must be "bundled with other non-electricity related variables including financial, technological, human capabilities, institutional, infrastructural, social, demographic" — and explicitly names the remaining research gaps. → [Conceptualization and antecedents of productive use of electricity](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8aafb1d57e395f7ea285cfa5cfd3e39d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Implication.** The originating memo's thesis — *green technology is not enough* — is not a novel claim. It is the settled prior. The contribution has to be the **magnitude of the gap and the ranking of what closes it**, not the existence of the gap.

## 2. The microenterprise-capital literature makes sharper predictions — and the canonical study is Ghanaian.

This is the more valuable body of work, and it was absent from the original framing. Green equipment *is* in-kind capital, so everything known about capital drops into microenterprises applies directly.

- **Fafchamps, McKenzie, Quinn & Woodruff (2013), Ghana** — cash vs. in-kind grants to urban microenterprises. **For women running subsistence enterprises, no profit gain from either treatment.** For women with larger businesses, only *in-kind* grants produced growth — the **flypaper effect**: capital that arrives as goods stays in the business; cash does not. → [Microenterprise growth and the flypaper effect](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8e73c1e5b65e5a8b9ef2b509754a8a9e/?utm_source=claude_desktop) · [companion](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6f75b4a811e1503a9569ec92a43d281c/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **de Mel, McKenzie & Woodruff (2008), Sri Lanka.** Returns to capital indistinguishable from zero for women. Notably, **men spent grants on working capital, women on equipment** — and it was the women who saw no returns. → [Are Women More Credit Constrained?](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b225da0e78f85b3d9580a7523716e0a9/?utm_source=claude_desktop) · [5-year follow-up in *Science*](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3d1a4e457b52561b95d54730219a361e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Field et al. (2019), re-analysis of Ghana, India and Sri Lanka.** The mechanism: women's returns were lower **only in households where another member also ran a business** — female capital was redirected to husbands' enterprises. Not an ability gap. → [Returns to Capital among Female Micro-Entrepreneurs in Ghana, India, and Sri Lanka](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7e4f32d7b23752dc93dcf83aa2103f11/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Berge, Bjorvatn & Tungodden (2015), Tanzania.** Grant × training factorial: strong effects for men, muted for women. Conclusion — "**business training is essential to transform financial capital into productive investments**." → [Human and Financial Capital for Microenterprise Development](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3fa1f260b5cc5c9c8f1713073bcec1ad/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Blattman et al. (2016), Uganda — the counterpoint.** $150 cash + 5 days training + **ongoing supervision** doubled enterprise ownership and incomes among ultrapoor women. When supervision was varied, it **increased business survival**. Bundles *can* work for women; supervision is a live ingredient. → [The Returns to Microenterprise Support among the Ultrapoor](https://consensus.app/papers/details/69edf59836de51a8aaa732e206240eb6/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Fiala (2018), Uganda.** Loans worked for men, grants did not; nothing for women. Repayment obligation appears to raise the odds of productive investment. → [Returns to microcredit, cash grants and training](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f06d62f18dd9587eb77381a284fc5441/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **de Mel et al. (2012), Sri Lanka.** Training alone: no profit impact. Training + grant: large gains that **dissipated in year two**. → [Business Training and Female Enterprise Start-Up, Growth, and Dynamics](https://consensus.app/papers/details/07b07667ce2b5e0bac3ecacf6c3f2753/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Two things this buys the proposal.**

**(a) A falsifiable prediction, not just a question.** Fafchamps et al. implies conversion should be **near zero below a threshold of baseline enterprise scale and positive above it** — a non-linearity the design can test directly. That moves the study from descriptive to theory-testing.

**(b) A specific, cheap-to-measure gender mechanism.** Field et al. predicts conversion failure concentrates in households containing **another enterprise**. One roster question captures it. This is far stronger than "gender norms matter," and it is exactly the kind of sharp secondary result that gets a paper published.

## 3. A serious measurement threat — and the fix is a second contribution.

- **Hing et al. (2023), rural India.** Compared sensor-recorded against survey-reported cookstove use. **28–46% of households over-reported.** Sensors showed most households had dis-adopted stoves *they had purchased with their own money*; **surveys failed to detect the decline entirely**, reporting adoption as unchanged. Some households with *zero* recorded use reported fuel savings and less smoke. → [Sensors show long-term dis-adoption of purchased improved cookstoves in rural India, while surveys miss it entirely](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b73b4b92f0245cdabe4991c86c9c8fc4/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Ruiz-Mercado et al. (2011)**, 461 citations — the canonical adoption-vs-sustained-use framework, arguing for objective monitoring over self-report. → [Adoption and sustained use of improved cookstoves](https://consensus.app/papers/details/903e73db122d5b19bc5b8756d2a570e0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Mekonnen et al. (2020), Ethiopia.** 63% of Mirt stoves still in place after 3.5 years; among abandoners, **over 80% stopped because of breakage**, not preference. Maintenance and after-sales, not motivation, drove disuse. → [Improved Biomass Cookstove use in the Longer Run](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4ba925b3a7095b188ce2a2edcc31680d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**This is the biggest risk to the design as drafted.** A proposal whose headline contribution is a conversion rate, measured by asking people, walks straight into a documented 28–46% over-reporting bias. A reviewer who knows this literature will say so.

**The fix is cheap and turns the threat into an asset.** Add **enumerator-verified use** alongside self-report: physical inspection that the equipment is present and functional, plus observable evidence of recent use — ash and residue and product on the racks for ovens, wear and connected load and charge-controller state for solar systems — with photographs. Then **report the discrepancy between verified and reported use as a finding**. That extends Hing et al. from household stoves to productive assets in an African setting, and it is publishable on its own.

## 4. Ghana-specific evidence is thin, dated, and leaves the enterprise question open.

- **Obeng et al. (2010), Ghana.** Solar PV and rural micro-enterprises across five regions — but the mechanism studied is **lighting and trading hours after sunset**, yielding $5–12/day in grocery enterprises. Fifteen years old, and not about processing or value addition. → [Impacts of public solar PV electrification on rural micro-enterprises: The case of Ghana](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b2172e61026b580c892f94bf2285942f/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Owusu et al. (2022), Ghana.** Electricity access and MSE profitability — but n=50, convenience sample, Accra only. Thin. → [Electricity Energy Access and Profitability of Micro and Small Enterprises in Ghana](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c0770706af91582994e25b82cf0dd1d3/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- **Ahotor oven.** Studied for **adoption drivers** (technology-acceptance framing — social influence, effort expectancy, habit) and for **product quality** (PAH levels against the Chorkor oven), but **not for enterprise outcomes**. Documented barriers: high upfront cost, and technical problems discouraging continued use among early adopters. → [Unraveling the drivers of adoption](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949824424000417) · [Oven type and smoked fish quality](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389022157_The_Effects_of_Oven_Type_on_the_Quality_of_Smoked_Fish_Comparison_Between_the_Use_of_the_Chorkor_and_Ahotor_Ovens_in_Ghana) · [SFMP market development report](https://www.crc.uri.edu/download/GH2014_ACT242_RESONANCE_FIN508.pdf)

**Implication.** The Ahotor gap is real and defensible: adoption and PAH are studied, value addition is not. And the PAH mechanism matters commercially, not just for health — Chorkor ovens produce PAH **7–10× above the EU standard**, so oven type plausibly gates access to formal and export buyers. That is a value-chain argument, which is what the ACET theme wants.

## 5. Closest scoop risk — cite it, differentiate from it.

**Owusu-Sekyere et al. (2024), Nigeria**, *Energy Policy*. 700 MSMEs, propensity-score matching: solar adoption associated with **23–27% higher monthly earnings** and **36.6% lower energy expenditure**. → [Impact of solar energy subscription on the market performance of MSMEs in Nigeria](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2db34509d6135883b6d066f6dd1c78dd/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

Same method family, adjacent country, published two years ago. **Name it in the concept note and differentiate**: they estimate adoption → earnings across general MSMEs; this study measures adoption → *conversion* → value addition within a defined agro-processing value chain, and ranks the constraints experimentally. Ignoring it looks like not knowing the field.

Also relevant on gender and PUE: **Pueyo et al. (2020), Tanzania** — men-owned enterprises use electricity more frequently and intensively; women dominate productive use of *cooking fuels*; and "in the absence of gender interventions, male entrepreneurs are more likely to benefit from the promotion of productive uses of energy." → [Exploring the linkages between energy, gender, and enterprise](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c8922341a0ba50acaa7f0637bf0b456a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

## 6. The policy hook the search turned up

Results-based financing in off-grid solar **pays companies per verified connection or device deployed**. The off-grid solar industry sold over 10 million kits in 2025, and roughly 645,000 households and farms now use solar pumps and refrigeration.

If a substantial share of deployed productive-use equipment never enters production, then **RBF is priced against the wrong metric**. That is a concrete, fundable policy argument with a named instrument attached — considerably sharper than "policymakers should consider complementary support."

### Literature-derived DCE attributes

Each attribute now carries a citation rather than being invented:

| Attribute | Why it is in the design |
|---|---|
| Capital form — in-kind vs. cash | Flypaper effect (Fafchamps et al. 2013) |
| Technical and business training | Training transforms capital into productive investment (Berge et al. 2015) |
| Ongoing supervision / follow-up | Supervision raised business survival (Blattman et al. 2016) |
| After-sales and maintenance service | 80% of abandonment was breakage (Mekonnen et al. 2020) |
| Market linkage / guaranteed buyer | Demand did not pick up on its own (Pueyo et al. 2018) |

A choice experiment whose attributes each answer a documented finding is far more defensible than one assembled from intuition.
