# Evidence from the SNV Ahotor Oven Performance Evaluation

Avega, B. and Tibu, G. A. (2017). *Performance Evaluation Survey: Ahotor Oven*. USAID/Ghana Sustainable Fisheries Management Project (SFMP), Coastal Resources Center, University of Rhode Island and SNV Netherlands Development Organisation. GH2014_ACT099_SNV, 35 pp.

Survey of 30 of the 33 Ahotor oven hosts, January 2017, across Elmina, Apam, Winneba, Mumford, Moree (Central Region) and Anlo Beach, Takoradi, Sekondi, Axim (Western Region).

This is the closest thing that exists to a baseline for the proposed study. It is a monitoring report rather than an evaluation of enterprise outcomes, which is exactly the gap the proposal fills, but it supplies numbers, mechanisms and an instrument.

---

## 1. The conversion gap is already documented in this population

The report records, without naming it as such, precisely the phenomenon the study proposes to measure.

- **36% of oven hosts had not used the Ahotor oven at all** at the time of the survey, months after installation. Concentrated in Moree.
- Of five respondents who owned only an Ahotor oven and no Chorkor, **three had never used it**.
- Only 40.7% had used it for three to five months; 22.6% for under two months.

The stated reason for non-use is the most valuable sentence in the document:

> "the main reason being **lack of capital to purchase fish**, and some received their stove components late."

Working capital, not training and not market access, is what the implementers observed blocking use. This is a strong prior for the choice experiment and it agrees with the Delphi consensus on finance in cold storage adoption.

## 2. Conversion failure in its purest form is recorded

> "Some of the women were using the Ahotor oven to smoke fish **for their home needs and not for the market**, because it gives them healthy fish."

Adopted, functional, used, and yet producing no marketed output. That is the conversion gap stated plainly by the implementing agency, and it justifies the entire framing.

## 3. Technology stacking is the norm, not the exception

The proposal treated stacking as a case worth investigating. This report shows it is the default.

- **64% were still using the Chorkor oven.** Only 3% had stopped.
- 25 of 30 respondents owned **both** Chorkor and Ahotor ovens.
- All respondents except two still used the Chorkor.
- Of 114 ovens owned across the sample, 73% were Chorkor units.

Reasons given: single-unit Ahotor capacity is too low; the Ahotor is slower unless preheated; the Chorkor takes 15 trays against the Ahotor's prescribed 10; "the Chorkor works better for hard smoking sessions."

Preference split: 43.3% preferred Ahotor, 23.3% preferred Chorkor, 33.3% gave no answer.

**Implication.** A binary adoption measure is meaningless here. The instrument must record the share of batches by oven type, by smoking mode (soft or hard) and by fish species.

## 4. Correction: the Ahotor oven does not reach EU compliance

The concept note claimed the oven establishes certification eligibility and thereby opens formal and export buyers. The measured figures do not support that.

| | PAH level |
|---|---|
| Chorkor oven | 298 µg/kg |
| Ahotor oven | 59 µg/kg |
| EU limit, PAH4 | 12 µg/kg |
| EU limit, benzo[a]pyrene | 2 µg/kg |

The Ahotor oven cuts PAH by about 80%, which is substantial. But at 59 µg/kg it remains roughly **five times the EU limit**. The report states the Chorkor runs about 25 times the standard, not the seven to ten times reported by secondary sources.

**This strengthens rather than weakens the study.** The technology closes most of the gap to compliance and still does not clear the threshold. That is the "technology is not enough" thesis with a hard number attached, and it means the certification pathway requires the oven plus something further: better fuel, cleaner combustion practice, handling, or a further design change. The proposal should ask what else is needed, not assert that the oven is sufficient.

## 5. The scale relationship is probably inverted-U, not a threshold

Hypothesis 1 followed Fafchamps et al. in predicting conversion rises with enterprise scale. This report points the other way at the top of the distribution:

> "Some respondents think that the Ahotor oven is for **small scale processors** because the **large processors' demand was not met** by the oven's capacity and performance."

So the binding constraint differs by scale. At the bottom it is working capital to buy fish. At the top it is the technical capacity of the oven itself. That predicts **an inverted U in conversion across enterprise scale**, which is a more interesting and more defensible hypothesis than a monotonic threshold, and it is grounded in field observation rather than extrapolated from a different country.

## 6. Willingness to pay falls well short of cost

| Item | Actual cost | Price respondents called realistic |
|---|---|---|
| Single unit | GHC 1,220 | GHC 100 to 700 |
| Double unit | GHC 2,330 | GHC 850 to 1,500 |
| Retrofit | | GHC 200 |

Payment preference: 37% would borrow from a finance institution, 10% would pay cash, 53% gave no answer. Named institutions include GN Bank, Opportunity International, Ahantaman Rural Bank, ASA Savings and Loans, Women's World Bank and susu companies.

**This calibrates the choice experiment.** The capital-form attribute should span the observed gap between GHC 700 and GHC 1,220 rather than using invented values.

## 7. Subsidy variation exists and can be exploited

The oven database records the subsidy rate for each unit. Almost all coastal ovens went out at **100% subsidy** to vulnerable-category women. A small number, in Takoradi and in the Ashanti sites at Konongo and Asomdwe Krom, went out at **30% subsidy**, meaning the recipient paid 70%.

That is naturally occurring variation in whether the recipient paid anything. It speaks directly to Fiala (2018), who found that repayment obligation raised the likelihood of productive investment, and to the flypaper effect in Fafchamps et al. If the numbers are large enough by now, comparing fully subsidised against part-paid adopters is a sharper test than anything currently in the design.

## 8. How ovens were obtained, and what that does to selection

- 52.4% received the oven under the **vulnerable household** category
- 38.1% through demonstration units
- One respondent purchased outright
- One was using a CEWEFIA demonstration unit

The existing adopter population is therefore selected on **vulnerability rather than entrepreneurial capacity**. This matters for external validity and it concentrates the sample precisely where Fafchamps predicts conversion is close to zero. It should be stated openly in the proposal.

## 9. A ready-made instrument and the units processors actually use

Appendix B of the report is the full evaluation questionnaire, which can be adapted rather than written from scratch.

More useful still, the report establishes the physical units processors think in, which is what the profit and value-added modules need:

- Daily capacity: **4 to 20 pans of fish**, depending on season
- One pan fills about **three smoking trays**
- At least **three bundles of fuelwood per pan**
- Soft smoking: 40 minutes to 3 hours on Chorkor, 1 to 3 hours on Ahotor
- Hard smoking: 5 to 8 hours on both
- Small pelagics are hard smoked; large fish such as tuna are soft smoked
- Transport to market: GHC 15 to 150 depending on distance and load
- Packaging: about GHC 25, covering paper, sacks, tying sticks, paint and brush for marking baskets
- Market tolls: GHC 1 to 5
- Processors substitute cold-store fish when sea landings are low

Fuel saving reported in practice is much larger than the laboratory figure: respondents said they use **about a third** of the fuelwood they use on the Chorkor, against a tested figure of 32% greater efficiency. That discrepancy between tested and perceived saving is itself worth measuring.

## 10. Two problems the proposal must now address

**Product mixing breaks price attribution.**

> "The women also mix the smoked fish from Chorkor with that from the Ahotor, thus there is no clear information on smoked fish consumer preference. There is therefore a need for a research in this direction."

If output from the two ovens is pooled before sale, a price premium cannot be attributed to the Ahotor at the point of sale. The design must either measure prices at batch level before mixing, or treat the premium as unidentifiable and rely on quantity, cost and buyer-type outcomes instead. Note also that SNV names consumer preference research as an explicit gap, which is a citable statement of need from the implementing agency.

**The population may be far too small.**

The database in the report lists roughly 36 ovens constructed to date across Volta, Central, Western and Ashanti regions. The proposal assumes 300 adopters. Unless dissemination scaled substantially after 2017, which the later SFMP market development work suggests was the intention, the sample is not there. **This is now the single most important thing to verify before submitting.**

## 11. Design faults that make after-sales a live hypothesis

Recorded by the survey team: the fat collector has sharp cut edges that injure users, disperses heat unevenly and slows cooking; fish in the middle tray does not cook through; the wood entrance is too small and set too high; the hood opening admits rodents, dogs, cats and goats; tray nets corrode from sea proximity; three users had been given no combustion grate at all.

The report's own recommendations lead with training, coaching and continuous monitoring, then financing. That is an implementer independently naming three of the five choice-experiment attributes.

## 12. Other things worth carrying

- SNV's 2014 stove enumeration found **over 120,000 fish smoking ovens** in near-constant use along the coast and the Volta Lake basin. A useful denominator for policy relevance.
- A third oven type exists. The **Morrison oven**, piloted at New Takoradi in 2008, is 40% more fuel efficient than the Chorkor, which is better than the Ahotor's 32%. The Ahotor's advantage is contaminant reduction, not fuel. The design should not assume a binary.
- Partner organisations that mobilised the hosts and hold the records: **CEWEFIA** (Central and Western Fish Traders Association, based in Elmina), **DAA** (Development Action Association), **DQF** (Daasgift Quality Foundation), **Hen Mpoano** (Takoradi), **Friends of the Nation**, and **SNV Ghana**. Each has post-harvest focal persons. Contact details are published in the front matter of the report.
- Fish spoils within 12 to 20 hours in tropical ambient temperatures, which is the underlying economics of the whole chain.
